# write-en-core

Default goal for English prose:
- preserve meaning
- cut filler
- reduce formulaic AI patterns
- keep rhythm natural
- match the reader and the job

## Core rules

### 1) Meaning first
- Do not make the sentence sound smarter if it becomes less precise.
- Keep factual boundaries, caveats, and technical conditions intact.

### 2) Cut filler fast
Remove or reduce phrases like:
- `it is worth noting that`
- `in today's fast-paced landscape`
- `moving forward`
- `this highlights the importance of`
- `as we can see`
- `in conclusion`

If the sentence still works without the phrase, delete it.

### 3) Avoid formulaic AI patterns
Reduce patterns like:
- `not X, but Y`
- rhetorical self-questions
- fake suspense (`here's the thing`)
- overbuilt tricolons
- bold claims without evidence
- punchy fragments used as drama

Prefer direct statements.

### 4) Prefer active, specific prose
- Name the actor when possible.
- Replace vague abstractions with the actual thing.
- Prefer `use` over `utilize`, `help` over `facilitate`, `show` over `demonstrate` when plain language is enough.

### 5) Trust the reader
- Do not announce that you are about to explain something.
- Do not summarize the same point three times.
- Do not over-soften obvious claims.

### 6) Keep rhythm human
- Mix sentence lengths.
- If every sentence has the same shape, break the pattern.
- Avoid em-dash abuse and over-punctuated emphasis.

## Anti-patterns

1. throat-clearing openings
2. fake authority tone
3. ornate synonyms where plain words work better
4. repeating the same argument in multiple phrasings
5. meta-writing (`in this section we will explore`)
6. overusing list labels when prose would be cleaner

## Quick preference
When in doubt, choose:
- clearer over cleverer
- shorter over puffed-up
- specific over abstract
- direct over theatrical
